# Overview

Keycard is open hardware for self-custody: a secure element smartcard that signs transactions on-chip, and Shell, a companion device that turns it into a full hardware wallet. Every layer is open source under MIT — the applet, the firmware, the schematics, the enclosure — and everything speaks standard protocols. If you can talk BIP-32, APDUs or animated QR codes, you can build on it.

## What is Keycard?

Keycard is a contactless smart card that stores private keys and performs signatures on a dedicated secure chip (NXP JCOP4, certified [EAL6+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evaluation_Assurance_Level)). It functions as a [BIP-32](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0032.mediawiki) HD wallet, supporting key generation, derivation and signing, and allows exporting keys defined in the context of [EIP-1581](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-1581). Keys never leave the card: the hash to sign goes in, the signature comes out.

The most obvious integration case is crypto wallets (ETH, BTC, etc.), but Keycard fits any system that uses a BIP-32 key tree or needs hardware-backed authentication and identification.

Keycard communicates via NFC and the ISO-7816 contact interface. Communication happens through a simple APDU interface, with a secure channel guaranteeing confidentiality, authentication and integrity of all commands. It's natively integrated with all [Status apps](https://status.app) and works on iOS/Android phones and on desktops with a USB smartcard reader.

## What is Keycard Shell?

[Keycard Shell](https://get.keycard.tech/pages/keycard-shell) is a full-fledged hardware wallet in which you insert your Keycard. It adds a keypad, a display, a camera and a USB port — and stays deliberately stateless: remove the card and the Shell forgets everything.

Shell enables air-gapped QR signing ([EIP-4527](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4527)) and on-device transaction verification with clear signing (ABI decoding, ERC-20 names, EIP-712, Bitcoin PSBT). This makes Keycard compatible with wallets like MetaMask, Rabby, Sparrow, BlueWallet and [many more](/wallets). Announced in [March 2025](/blog/announcing-keycard-shell), it evolved from a design journey starting in 2022 ([Keycard Origins](/blog/keycard-origins-shells-design-journey-from-concept-to-prototype)).

## Why build on it?

**Everything is open.** The [applet](https://github.com/keycard-tech/status-keycard), the [Shell firmware, schematics, BOM and 3D files](https://github.com/keycard-tech/keycard-shell) are all public and MIT licensed. Keycard Shell is the only multi-chain hardware wallet with fully open hardware. There is nothing you cannot inspect, fork or reuse.

**Standard protocols only.** BIP-32/39 key management, ISO-7816 APDUs, EIP-4527 and [Blockchain Commons UR](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/ur/) QR encoding, PSBT for Bitcoin, EIP-712 typed data. No proprietary formats, no vendor lock-in: an integration you write against Keycard follows the same standards as the rest of the ecosystem.

**The secure element is the signer.** Unlike most hardware wallets, keys are not exported to a general-purpose MCU for signing — all cryptographic operations run inside the EAL6+ certified chip, and the applet is immutable once the card is locked. The host (phone, desktop, Shell) never sees key material.

**People you may know already trust it.** Keycard Shell ranks [#1 on Wallet Beat](https://walletbeat.eth.limo/hww/summary/) and scored [93% on hardware-wallets.net](https://www.hardware-wallets.net/keycard-shell-review/). Ledger's co-founder called it ["a hardware wallet security designed the right way"](https://x.com/BTChip/status/1790748671291621651), Safe's CEO ["an amazing piece of open-source hardware"](https://x.com/SchorLukas/status/1986013483352383929) — and even [Keystone's CTO agrees](https://x.com/aaron1sme/status/2021526484800925741).

## Choose your path

**Integrate Keycard into your wallet or app.** Start with [Getting Started](/developers/getting-started) and [Core Concepts](/developers/core), then use the [Java SDK](/developers/sdk) (desktop and Android). SDKs also exist for Go, Swift, Python, TypeScript and more — see the [ecosystem page](/developers/github-repositories). If you work in another language, the [APDU protocol reference](/developers/apdu) documents everything you need to implement it yourself.

**Understand how it works.** [Core Concepts](/developers/core) covers the card lifecycle and credentials model, [Keycard Shell internals](/developers/keycard-shell) covers the QR formats, USB protocol, clear signing database and firmware security, and the [hardware specification](/developers/hardware-specification) lists the exact chips and certifications.

**Build your own hardware.** The applet [builds on any compatible JavaCard](/developers/build), and you can assemble a [DIY Keycard Shell](/developers/diy-keycard-shell) from the published schematics and firmware.

**Contribute.** From adding contract ABIs (better clear signing for everyone) to porting SDKs — see [how to contribute](/developers/contribute).

## Versioning

The current applet version is listed in the [status-keycard releases](https://github.com/keycard-tech/status-keycard/releases). This documentation applies to the current protocol version. [Semantic versioning](https://semver.org) is used throughout the project, with the omission of the patch number for the applet, but not the SDK and related tools.
